## Your browser is your most-used app

The browser is where most of your online life happens — banking, work, messaging, shopping. It's also where most attacks land. A few small choices make a big difference.

## Use a modern browser, kept up to date

Pick one of: Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Brave, or Safari. Avoid old browsers like Internet Explorer or any browser bundled into outdated software. Modern browsers ship security fixes constantly — let them auto-update and **restart your browser at least once a week** so updates apply.

## Always check the address bar

Before typing a password or payment info, look at the address bar:

- The site is **HTTPS** (a lock icon, no warnings).
- The domain is **exactly right**. `paypal.com` is real. `paypa1.com` and `paypal-secure.com` and `paypal.com.example.io` are not.
- You typed it yourself or used a bookmark, rather than following a link from email or chat.

Browsers occasionally show a red full-screen warning for known phishing or malware. **Believe it.** Do not click "Proceed anyway".

## Useful extensions (a small, careful set)

Extensions can read everything you do in the browser, so install only what you need from publishers you trust. A small set worth considering:

- **Password manager extension** — for autofill and breach detection.
- **uBlock Origin** — open-source ad and tracker blocker. Reduces malicious-ad risk and noise.
- **Privacy Badger** — blocks third-party trackers automatically.
- **HTTPS-only mode** — built into most browsers; turn it on to block plain HTTP.

Avoid random "speed booster" or "coupon finder" extensions. Many are quiet trackers.

## Cookies, trackers, and fingerprinting

Modern privacy controls go far beyond cookies. To raise the floor:

- Set your browser to **block third-party cookies**.
- Turn on the browser's **anti-tracking** feature (Brave Shields, Firefox Enhanced Tracking Protection, Safari Intelligent Tracking Prevention).
- Use **separate browser profiles** or containers for work, personal, and shopping. This stops cross-profile tracking and reduces the blast radius if one profile is compromised.

## Downloads and pop-ups

- **Don't download what you didn't ask for.** A site offering a "missing video codec" or a "system scan" is almost always malware.
- **Read installer screens** — bundled "extras" are often unwanted software.
- **Allow browser notifications only for sites you trust.** Notification spam is a common scam channel.

## Public Wi-Fi

Modern HTTPS makes public Wi-Fi safer than it used to be, but a few habits still help:

- Avoid logging into sensitive accounts on networks you don't trust.
- Verify the network name with the venue rather than trusting whatever your phone suggests.
- A reputable VPN adds another layer when you have to use public networks regularly.
- At home, change the default admin password on your router and keep its firmware up to date.

## What to do today

- [ ] Update your browser and restart it.
- [ ] Install uBlock Origin and your password manager extension.
- [ ] Turn on HTTPS-only mode and third-party cookie blocking.
- [ ] Bookmark your top 10 sites and use the bookmarks instead of typing or searching.
- [ ] Audit your installed extensions and remove anything you don't recognize.

> **Bottom line:** keep your browser current, double-check the address bar, install only the extensions you need, and treat unexpected downloads or pop-ups as suspicious by default.
