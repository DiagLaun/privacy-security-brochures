## Why passwords still matter

Most account breaches do not start with elite hackers. They start with a leaked password from one website being reused on another. If your email password is the same as your password on a forum that got breached in 2019, attackers will eventually try it on your inbox.

The two rules that protect you against almost everything:

1. **Every account gets its own unique password.**
2. **Every password is long enough that guessing it is not realistic.**

You cannot follow both rules with your memory alone. That is what password managers are for.

## What makes a password strong?

Length beats complexity. A 4-word random passphrase like `correct-staple-orchid-river` is far harder to crack than `P@ssw0rd1!`, even though the second one looks "complex".

Aim for:

- **At least 14 characters**, ideally 16+
- **Random** — generated by a password manager, not chosen by you
- **Unique** — never reused across accounts

If you must create a password by hand (e.g. for your password manager itself), use a 5–6 word random passphrase from a dice-generated word list.

## Use a password manager

A password manager generates, stores, and fills passwords for you. Good options include 1Password, Bitwarden (open source, free tier), Proton Pass, and the manager built into your browser or operating system.

What you get:

- Unique strong passwords for every site, automatically
- Autofill so logging in is faster than reusing one password
- A breach monitor that warns you when a saved password leaks
- Secure sharing for family or team passwords

The one password you do need to remember is the **master password** for the manager itself. Make that one a long passphrase you have never used anywhere else.

## Protect the master password and recovery

Your password manager is now the most valuable account you own. Treat it accordingly:

- Turn on two-factor authentication on the manager.
- Save your recovery key or emergency kit in a safe place — printed on paper in a drawer is fine for most people.
- Never type your master password into any site you reached through a link in an email.

## What to do today

- [ ] Pick a password manager and install it on every device you use.
- [ ] Create a long, unique master passphrase.
- [ ] Turn on 2FA for the password manager.
- [ ] Import or save your existing passwords; let the manager flag the weak and reused ones.
- [ ] Replace the top 5 weakest or reused passwords with generated ones — start with email, banking, and primary social accounts.

> **Bottom line:** you do not need to memorize good passwords. You need a password manager, one strong master passphrase, and the discipline to never reuse passwords across sites.
