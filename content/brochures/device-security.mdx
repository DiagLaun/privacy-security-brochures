## Your devices are the front door

A weak device makes everything else weaker. If someone can pick up your unlocked laptop or phone, your password manager, email, and 2FA codes are all theirs. The good news: most devices ship with strong protections built in. You just have to turn them on.

## The five non-negotiables

### 1. Lock screen with a strong code

Use:

- A **6+ digit PIN** (not 1234, not your birth year, not your address number)
- A **passphrase** if you can tolerate typing it
- **Biometrics** (Face ID, Touch ID, Windows Hello) on top — fast and convenient

Set the screen to lock automatically after **1–2 minutes** of inactivity. Lock the screen manually when you walk away from a laptop in a shared space.

### 2. Full-disk encryption

If your device is lost or stolen, encryption is what keeps your files unreadable to a stranger. It is built into every modern OS:

- **macOS** — FileVault. On by default for newer devices; verify in System Settings → Privacy & Security.
- **Windows** — BitLocker (Pro / Enterprise) or Device Encryption (Home).
- **iOS / Android** — automatically encrypted when you set a passcode.
- **Linux** — LUKS (set up at install time).

Save the recovery key somewhere safe — your password manager is fine, an account-linked recovery is fine — just *not* on the same drive.

### 3. Automatic updates

Most exploited bugs are ones with patches that nobody installed. Turn on auto-updates and reboot regularly:

- OS updates
- App / browser updates
- Firmware updates (your laptop, your router, your TV — yes, your TV)

Aim to install OS updates within a week of release.

### 4. Backups

Hardware fails. Devices get stolen. Files get encrypted by ransomware. The fix is backups, not hope.

A reasonable home setup:

- **Cloud backup** for everyday files (iCloud, OneDrive, Google Drive, or Backblaze)
- **Local backup** to an external drive once a week (Time Machine on macOS, File History on Windows)
- **Test a restore** at least once. A backup you have never verified is not really a backup.

### 5. Find My / device tracking

Turn on the OS-level "find my device" feature for every phone, tablet, and laptop you own. It lets you:

- Locate the device on a map
- Lock it remotely
- Wipe it remotely if you can't recover it

Make sure you know the password to your tracking account — it is what you'll use the day you actually need it.

## Bonus: harden the basics

Once the five above are done, a few extras add a lot:

- **Don't run as administrator day-to-day** on Windows or Linux. Use a standard account and elevate when needed.
- **Review installed apps** every few months. Uninstall anything you don't use.
- **Turn off Bluetooth and AirDrop / Quick Share** when you aren't using them, especially in public spaces.
- **Keep your router updated** and change its default admin password. Replace any router that no longer gets firmware updates.
- **For travel,** consider a separate "travel" laptop or phone with only the data you actually need on it.

## What to do today

- [ ] Set or upgrade your device PIN/passphrase. Enable biometrics.
- [ ] Confirm full-disk encryption is on.
- [ ] Turn on automatic updates and install any pending ones.
- [ ] Set up at least one form of backup, and test it.
- [ ] Enable Find My / find-my-device.

> **Bottom line:** a locked, encrypted, auto-updating device with backups is dramatically harder to attack — and the recovery story is dramatically better when something does go wrong.
